# Evidence map: rampwise-aerobic-base

Source names refer to `library.md` in this folder. "Verified" = read in the abstract or full text (2026-11-16).
Strength: **research** = trial/meta-analysis/large dataset; **practice** = coaching convention without a study in this library; **gap** = unsupported.

| Rule or output | What the source says (verified) | Strength | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Decoupling / cardiovascular drift is a usable marker of training response (trend, standardised test) | Barsumyan 2025 (20 well-trained cyclists, one 60-min ride at 75 % of functional threshold power per month for 5 months): drift and aerobic decoupling correlated strongly; models classified responders vs non-responders with 87-93 % cross-validated accuracy; authors conclude both are reliable indicators of response to training. | research (small, cyclists) | Supports using a **standardised** repeated test. Full text in `papers/` |
| Decoupling reflects fatigue, not only fitness | Barsumyan 2026 (17 trained cyclists, 85 paired tests): cadence decline in the second half correlated with drift (r about 0.40) and decoupling (r about 0.38); each extra rpm of decline went with about 0.6 % more drift. | research (small) | Used for the cadence line on rides with a cadence sensor |
| Heat, fluids and fuel also drive decoupling | Wang 2026 (13 adolescent runners, simulated half marathon): HR-speed decoupling rose progressively; earlier onset went with larger core-temperature rise and sodium loss per km; late-run decoupling went with glucose change. | research (small, adolescents) | Why the coach checks weather and fuelling before blaming fitness |
| Cardiac cost (HR / speed) tracks drift in marathons and depends on pacing | Billat 2019 (280 sub-elite marathoners): 77 % slowed from about km 26 ("fallers"); fallers had lower performance and higher cardiac drift; recommends cardiac cost (HR divided by speed). | research (observational) | Same idea as EF; inverse |
| Cardiovascular drift definition | Coyle and Gonzalez-Alonso 2001 (review, abstract only): a progressive fall in stroke volume after 10-20 min of exercise, accompanied by rising heart rate. | research (review) | |
| Durability: efficiency changes during prolonged exercise matter and differ between athletes | Maunder 2021 (review): physiological-profile values measured fresh shift during prolonged exercise; proposes quantifying "durability" as time of onset and size of deterioration. Smyth 2022 (82,303 marathoners, full text): internal-to-external workload decoupling; low-decoupling group (34.5 %) ran faster overall and began decoupling later (about km 33 vs 23 vs 19). Grivas 2026 (narrative review): late-race collapse ("the wall") is multifactorial (metabolic, thermoregulatory, dehydration, GI, neuromuscular, pacing). | research | Basis for the durability line on 90+ min sessions |
| Speed at a fixed heart rate (or HR at a fixed speed) tracks aerobic adaptation, **if the test is standardised** | Vesterinen 2016 (abstract): 35 endurance-trained adults, 18 weeks, a weekly 3-stage submaximal run test (stages at 70, 80 and 90 % of max HR): running speed in every stage correlated r = .60-.89 with VO2max, peak running speed and lactate-threshold speeds; changes in speed at the 80 % and 90 % stages tracked the change in peak speed (r = .57, .79). Vesterinen 2017 (abstract): speed at the 90 % stage was the most sensitive variable; the top responders improved speed in the 80 % and 90 % stages, the lowest responders did not. Buchheit 2014 (full text, rampwise-recovery): submaximal exercise HR is within-athlete a marker of relative intensity (lower HR = fitter) and large correlations with performance gains were reported; it needs at least 3-4 min to reach steady state and the last 30-60 s is used. | research (small, endurance-trained, protocol tests) | Supports "speed at a given HR" as a trend **within the athlete under repeatable conditions**; not shown for free-form runs. |
| A lower HR at the same effort is not always a fitter athlete | Ten Haaf 2019 (abstract): 30 amateur cyclists around an 8-day event: HR at fixed submaximal workloads fell by about 4-6 bpm after intensified training but was unrelated to the change in performance; the authors conclude submaximal HR seems inadequate to prescribe and monitor intensified training. Buchheit 2014: a raised HR should not be used alone as a marker of fatigue or fitness loss. | research (small) | Read HR-at-pace together with sleep/HRV and load, never alone. |
| HR drift at a fixed pace is larger in the heat | Lafrenz 2008 (abstract): in 10 trained men, cardiovascular drift and the fall in VO2max were greater at 35 C than at 22 C during 45 min of cycling at about 59 % VO2max. | research (small, cycling, lab) | Compare "how long I hold the pace" only between sessions at similar temperature. |
| "How long can I hold the pace inside my HR zone" as one number | **No paper read defines or validates this exact measure.** Closest verified ideas: the *onset* of drift/decoupling as a durability marker (Maunder 2021 review; Smyth 2022: runners who began decoupling later ran faster) and a fixed-power 60-min drift test in cyclists (Barsumyan 2025). The zone ceiling itself depends on how the zones were set (Jamnick 2020: %HRmax anchors are weak). | gap (concept supported, metric not validated) | **Built as `hold_check.py` (2026-11-16), labelled practice**: within-athlete trend only, compared with session-to-session variation; thresholds (+/-10 % pace, 60 s crossing, 15 bpm band, 10 min skip, 2 % flat) are working rules, not published. |
| 5 % / 10 % decoupling and 3 % / 6 % durability cut-offs | **No paper in this library sets these thresholds.** 5 % is a long-standing coaching convention (Friel / TrainingPeaks). | practice | Used only as labels inside the athlete's own trend |
| Most training time easy | Seiler 2010 (review): elite endurance athletes self-organise to about 80 % of sessions at low intensity and about 20 % high-intensity; extra high-intensity emphasis in already well-trained athletes showed no convincing long-term gain. Muniz-Pumares 2025 (151,813 marathons, abstract): faster runners trained over three times more volume, mainly more easy (Z1) running; pyramidal distribution most common, especially in the fastest. | research (review + large observational) | Direction is consistent: volume and easy share |
| Which distribution is best is not settled | Stoggl and Sperlich 2014 (RCT, 48 well-trained athletes, 9 weeks): polarised gave the largest gain in VO2peak (+11.7 %) and most key variables vs threshold, high-intensity-interval or high-volume. Rosenblat 2019 (meta-analysis, 3 trials): polarised beat threshold on time trial (ES -0.66, 95 % CI -1.17 to -0.15), but studies were low quality (PEDro 4-5/10). Li 2026 (Bayesian network meta-analysis): no model showed a definite advantage over polarised (all 95 % intervals crossed zero); threshold ranked best for VO2max, high-intensity for time trial; "tailor to athlete and sport". | research (mixed) | Do not claim one model wins |
| HR zones from percentages of max HR are weak | Jamnick 2020 (review): prescribing intensity from a fixed percentage of maximal anchors (VO2max, HRmax, max work rate) "has little merit" for producing distinct physiological responses; submaximal anchors (lactate/ventilatory thresholds) are better. | research (review) | Why the skill asks whether zones came from a test |
| EF and "higher = fitter" | Concept appears in the upstream repo (TrainingPeaks). No paper in this library validates a numeric EF target. | practice | Compare within athlete only |
| Steady-state needed for decoupling; skip warm-up | Convention in the upstream script (10 min); Barsumyan used a fixed-power 60-min protocol. The reliability flags (speed variation, hills) are this skill's own working rules. | practice | Flags exist so the script says "no valid reading" when appropriate |

## Not supported (do not claim)
- A specific healthy decoupling number as a medical or injury indicator.
- That one training-intensity distribution is proven best for this athlete.
- A "Zone 2 heart rate" derived from age or percent of max.
- Efficiency conclusions from speed:HR on hilly or stop-and-go rides without power.
- Ultra-cycling-specific intensity prescriptions (no such trial in this library).
